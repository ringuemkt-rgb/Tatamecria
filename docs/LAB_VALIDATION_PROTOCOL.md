# Laboratory Validation Protocol

## Goal

Determine whether selected NeuroJitsu metrics are sufficiently reliable and accurate for a **specific standardized task**, not whether the software is “valid in general.”

## Recommended staged design

### Stage 1 — task definition

For each task define:

- start/end criteria;
- repetitions;
- participant position;
- camera location;
- lighting;
- clothing constraints if any;
- reference instrument;
- synchronization method;
- primary metric(s);
- invalidation criteria.

### Stage 2 — reference comparison

Candidate comparisons:

- video joint angle vs reference kinematics;
- video trunk inclination vs reference kinematics;
- video trajectory features vs reference trajectory;
- video postural features vs force/pressure-platform outcomes.

Reference systems and exact outcomes must be selected by the host laboratory.

### Stage 3 — reliability

Estimate, where appropriate:

- test–retest ICC;
- within-session and between-day error;
- SEM;
- MDC;
- invalid/missing rate.

### Stage 4 — agreement

Estimate, where appropriate:

- mean bias;
- Bland–Altman limits of agreement;
- MAE/RMSE;
- proportional bias;
- heteroscedasticity.

Statistical choices and acceptable thresholds must be pre-specified with the supervising statistician.

### Stage 5 — robustness

Repeat under controlled perturbations:

- camera distance;
- camera height;
- camera angle;
- lighting;
- partial occlusion;
- clothing contrast.

### Stage 6 — grappling feasibility

Only after single-participant validation:

- two adults;
- low-risk standardized positions;
- standing and ground tasks;
- track uncertainty;
- occlusion completeness;
- single vs two-camera comparison.

## Success does not mean “clinical validation”

Even excellent technical agreement in adults does not establish validity in autistic children. Population-specific reliability, feasibility, acceptability and ethics remain separate questions.
